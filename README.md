# Economic Cycle Identification

> **A macro framework for answering the question: “Where the hell are we in the economic cycle?”**
> Tracks key metrics across four phases and translates them into market implications because apparently staring at 47 charts wasn't enough.

---

##  What It Is

**Economic Cycle Identification** is a macroeconomic framework designed to identify the current phase of the economic cycle by combining multiple economic and financial indicators.

Instead of relying on a single indicator, the framework looks at the broader picture across **growth, employment, inflation, monetary policy, credit, the yield curve, and business activity**.

The cycle is divided into four broad regimes:

**Early Cycle → Mid Cycle → Late Cycle → Recession**

---

##  Why It Exists

Economic data rarely tells the same story at the same time.

One indicator can be improving while another is deteriorating. Some indicators lead the economy, while others confirm what already happened.

This project attempts to organize that information into a structured framework that can answer:

* Where are we in the economic cycle?
* Which indicators are confirming the current regime?
* Are multiple indicators beginning to transition?
* What could the current regime imply for major asset classes?

The goal is **not to predict the future with a crystal ball**.

It's to create a consistent way of reading the macro environment.

---

##  Framework

The framework divides the economic cycle into four phases:

| Phase              | Environment | General Characteristics                                    |
| ------------------ | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 🟢 **Early Cycle** | Recovery    | Growth recovering, policy easing, credit improving         |
| 🔵 **Mid Cycle**   | Expansion   | Strong growth, healthy credit, broad risk appetite         |
| 🟡 **Late Cycle**  | Slowdown    | Growth deteriorating, inflation sticky, policy restrictive |
| 🔴 **Recession**   | Contraction | Growth falling, unemployment rising, aggressive easing     |

### The key idea

> **Transitions matter more than labels.**

A single indicator changing direction may not mean much.

When several indicators begin transitioning between regimes simultaneously, the signal becomes considerably more interesting.

##  How To Use It

The framework can be used as a **macro regime filter** rather than an intraday trading system.

### Step 1 — Evaluate the indicators

Classify each indicator according to the economic phase it currently resembles.

### Step 2 — Identify the dominant regime

Look for the phase receiving the strongest overall confirmation.

### Step 3 — Watch for transitions

Pay particular attention when multiple indicators begin moving from one phase toward another.

### Step 4 — Apply the macro bias

Use the identified regime to establish a broader directional bias for the assets being monitored.

> **Macro tells you the environment. It doesn't tell you where to click Buy or Sell.**

---

##  Asset Implications

Different economic regimes can create very different environments across asset classes.

###  Early Cycle

Generally favorable for:

* Equities
* Small Caps
* Cyclicals
* High-Yield Credit

Gold may become less dominant as risk appetite returns.

###  Mid Cycle

Generally associated with:

* Strong equity performance
* Broad risk appetite
* Healthy credit conditions
* Stronger commodities

###  Late Cycle

Potentially favors:

* Defensive equities
* Gold
* US Treasuries

While cyclicals and higher-risk credit may begin to weaken.

###  Recession

Typically characterized by:

* Equity weakness
* Credit stress
* Falling commodities
* Stronger demand for defensive assets
* Aggressive monetary easing

These are **regime-level tendencies, not guaranteed outcomes**.

---

##  Limitations

This framework has several important limitations.

* Economic data is released with different frequencies and lags.
* Indicators can give conflicting signals.
* Economic regimes do not change cleanly from one phase to another.
* Historical relationships can break down.
* Market prices are influenced by expectations, positioning, liquidity, and events beyond the economic cycle.
* The framework should not be treated as a standalone trading system.
* Asset behavior can vary significantly from one cycle to another.

**In short: the economy doesn't read the README.**
